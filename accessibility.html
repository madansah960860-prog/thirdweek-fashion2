Accessibility

Everyone gets the same shop

Last updated September 8, 2026 · Applies to www.vestiary.co

We want this site to work for everybody, and we would rather hear about a barrier than have somebody quietly give up on an order.

What this page covers WCAG 2.1 Level AA Report acknowledged in 2 days Plan within 10 Orders taken by phone

Six sections follow: the standard we hold ourselves to, the things already built into every page, the three limitations we know about and have not solved yet, the assistive technology we test with, how to tell us something is broken and what we do when you do, and how to order without using the website at all. The last two carry commitments with dates attached, so they are the ones to read if you are short of time.

Our target

We aim to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1 at Level AA. That is the standard referenced by the Americans with Disabilities Act guidance and by Section 508, and it is the level we test against.

We consider this site to be substantially conformant. Substantially, rather than fully, because we test manually rather than through a certified audit, and because the honest position is that no site is finished.

What is built in

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses real landmarks: a header, a main region, a footer and labelled navigation. Headings run in order without skipped levels.
  • Skip to content. The first focusable element on every page is a skip link that jumps straight to the main content.
  • Keyboard access. Everything that can be clicked can be reached and operated with a keyboard alone: the menu, the dropdowns, the product filters, the gallery thumbnails, the FAQ accordions, the cookie banner and every form.
  • Visible focus. A three-pixel focus ring in ember appears on every focusable element. It is never removed, including on custom controls.
  • Contrast. Body text sits at roughly sixteen to one against the background. All interactive text and all large text exceed the 4.5 to 1 and 3 to 1 minimums. The ember accent is used for short labels and rules, never as small print over a competing ground.
  • Alt text on every image. Descriptive rather than decorative: what the garment is, how it is laid out and what is visible in the frame.
  • Touch targets. Interactive controls are at least forty-four pixels in the smaller dimension, with spacing between them.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label above it, not a placeholder standing in for one. Errors appear inline beneath the field they belong to, are announced to assistive technology, and describe how to fix the problem.
  • Reduced motion. The hairline sweep, the plate reveals and every transition are disabled when your system requests reduced motion. Nothing on this site loops, flashes or moves on its own.
  • No motion or audio on load. There is no autoplaying video, no background audio and no carousel that advances by itself.
  • Zoom and reflow. Text can be resized to 200 percent and the layout reflows to a single column without horizontal scrolling. Pinch zoom is not disabled.
  • No interruptions. The cookie banner sits at the bottom of the page and does not block content. There are no entry interstitials and no pop-ups.
  • Tables. Every size chart has a caption, real header cells with scope attributes, and horizontal scrolling contained inside the table rather than the page.

Known limitations

Three things we know about and are working on.

  • Colour description in product records. Colour is described in words on every record, but some descriptions are more precise than others. We are rewriting them so that anybody buying without seeing the image gets the same information.
  • Long size tables on small screens. The tables scroll horizontally inside their container on narrow viewports. That works, but a stacked layout would be easier with a screen magnifier and we intend to build one.
  • Plates are dense. A full-resolution plate of a knit contains a lot of visual information that alt text can only summarise. Where the detail matters, the record text describes it in words, and you can always ask us for a written description of any image.

What we test with

Manual testing is carried out with VoiceOver on macOS and iOS, NVDA on Windows, keyboard-only navigation in Chrome, Firefox and Safari, and at browser zoom levels of 200 and 400 percent. We also test the whole site at 360 pixels wide, which is where layout problems show up first.

Tell us what is broken

If any part of this site is difficult or impossible to use, please tell us. Include the page, what you were trying to do, and the browser and assistive technology you were using if you know them. We acknowledge accessibility reports within two business days and give a plan and a timeframe within ten.

Email access@vestiary.co, call (503) 555-0192 between 9:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. Pacific Time, or write to Vestiary Supply Co., 218 NW Couch Street, Suite 4A, Portland, OR 97209, United States.

Ordering without the site

If the website is not workable for you for any reason, we will take an order by telephone or email and read out any measurement, condition note or description you need. There is no difference in price, in shipping or in return rights. Nothing on this site is only available online.

A report is not a complaint. You do not need to know which guideline was missed, name a success criterion, or prove anything. One sentence about what would not work is enough to start the clock on the two-day acknowledgement above, and we will do the diagnosis at our end. Reports from people using this site are how most of the list above got written in the first place.
