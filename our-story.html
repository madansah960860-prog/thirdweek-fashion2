Origin

Two rooms and a light stand

Vestiary started in 2023 in a two-room walk-up off Burnside in Portland, with a folding table, a borrowed light stand and a spreadsheet. Two of us had been reselling secondhand clothing for years on general marketplaces, and we had spent all of that time on the wrong side of the same failure. A garment would photograph beautifully on a hanger and arrive two inches shorter than the buyer expected. A mended cuff would vanish into the shadow under a sleeve. Nobody was lying. The picture simply was not carrying the information.

The fix turned out to be geometry rather than equipment. We laid a jacket face down on a white plate, raked a single light across it from the left, and shot it from directly overhead. The weave came up. The fade line came up. The repair we had made at the elbow the previous week came up, clearly, in a way that a styled photograph would have flattered out of existence.

We named the studio after the room a household keeps its clothes in. A vestiary holds what already exists. It does not manufacture anything.

The first drop went out in March 2024: nineteen pieces to a mailing list of about eighty people, most of them friends. It cleared in four days, which taught us less than we assumed at the time. The April drop sold roughly a third and taught us considerably more. People will buy a garment they have never touched, but only when the measurements are on the page before the price is.

So we rebuilt the listing process around the measured note. Every piece gets a record card filled in at the bench before it ever reaches the plate: chest, sleeve, length, weight, gauge. The website publishes those cards. Everything else on a listing is secondary to them.

There are three of us now, working out of a suite on NW Couch Street. Roughly sixty seasonless pieces sit in the register at any moment, year round, and we intend to keep it near that number. The method only holds because a person handles every garment twice.

Method

How the business runs

No investors, no wholesale middle layer, no consignment inbound. We buy the clothes outright, we own them, and we sell them to you directly in US dollars.

  • Buy in bulk, refuse most of it

    Estate clear-outs, rag house lots and a handful of long-term sellers in Oregon and Washington. Roughly one piece in six survives the sorting table.

  • Clean first, repair second

    Wet cleaning, hand washing or dry cleaning depending on the fibre, then whatever mending the piece needs. Repairs are photographed, never hidden.

  • Measure by hand, then plate it

    Five to nine measurements written into the record, then four exposures shot flat under raking light. The record card becomes the listing.

  • Listed once, sold once, retired

    One unit per plate number. When it sells the listing closes and the number is never reused. There is no restock because there is nothing to restock.

Intake runs about three weeks end to end: washing, mending, measuring and plating, in that order, with the record card travelling alongside the garment the whole way. New work is published on the first Friday of the month at 11:00 a.m. Pacific Time.

Pricing

Four inputs build a price: what we paid at intake, the direct cost of cleaning and repair, the labour of measuring and plating, and scarcity in that particular size. There is no markdown calendar. If a piece has not sold in ninety days we re-photograph it and reconsider the price once, permanently. No sale event, no flash discount, no countdown, because a coat that fits you in April still fits you in November.

Shipping and returns

Orders of $125 or more ship free anywhere in the fifty states. Below that it is a flat $8 regardless of weight. You have thirty days from delivery to return or exchange. We pay return shipping on exchanges and on anything we described wrongly. On a change-of-mind refund, an $8 label fee comes out of the refund. All of it is set out in full on the shipping page and the returns page.

Gift cards and referrals

Digital gift cards come in fixed amounts, never expire, and are redeemable against anything on the site including made-to-order pieces. The referral programme gives both people a store credit once a referred order has shipped and cleared the return window. Full terms sit on the gift cards and referrals page.

Wholesale and stockists

A small number of independent shops carry selections from the register. Because every piece is a single unit, wholesale works as a consigned capsule rather than a repeatable order sheet. If you run a shop, the stockists page sets out the terms and carries the enquiry form.

Made to order

The Re-cut line turns garments too damaged to sell into vests, caps, mittens and overshirts, with published lead times of two to eight weeks depending on the pattern. Details are on the new arrivals page.

Limits

What we will not do

There is no countdown timer anywhere on this site and there never will be. No "only two left" banners either, because there is only ever one of anything, and saying so in red type would be manipulation rather than information.

We do not publish customer reviews or star ratings. Not for lack of good ones, but because a one-of-one register makes reviews structurally misleading: nobody reading a review of VS-0431 can ever buy VS-0431. If we add reviews later they will be about service, clearly labelled, and verified against real orders.

We do not dye leather to hide wear. We do not replace a full button set to make something look newer. We do not photograph a garment in a way that flatters a flaw out of existence. If a piece has a paint mark on the hip, the paint mark is on the plate and in the condition line.

We also do not claim to be solving fashion's environmental problem. Selling used clothing is better than manufacturing new clothing, and that is roughly the size of the claim we are willing to make. The materials page sets out what we do and where the limits are.
