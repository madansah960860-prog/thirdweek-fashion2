Why the plate

No models, by design

A model wearing a coat tells you how that coat sits on that person. It tells you almost nothing about how it will sit on you, and it hides the things that decide a secondhand purchase: the fade line across a shoulder, the crease at an elbow, the repaired cuff, the exact drop of a hem.

So the lookbook is made the way the listings are, flat on the plate with one lamp raking across the surface. What you lose is the fantasy. What you gain is a garment at the same scale as the one already hanging in your wardrobe.

The looks are real combinations, assembled from stock that existed on one afternoon. We put them together to answer the question that arrives most often, which is not what does this look like but what does this go with.

Where a piece has already sold we say so rather than quietly substituting a replacement. That is the point of a single-run wardrobe: this is a record of a moment, not a standing catalogue.

No bodies. Only evidence.

Look 02

The working week

Chore jacket, Shetland crewneck, bridle belt. The three pieces we would keep if we had to keep three.

The jacket runs generous, so the Shetland goes underneath without pulling anywhere. Size the jacket down to a medium and the merino turtleneck is the better layer.

Jacket record · Crewneck record · Belt record

Look 03

Shoulder season

Cotton boatneck, straight denim, field boots. What most of us actually wear for eight months of the year in Oregon.

The boatneck carries this one. A wide, shallow neckline reads as deliberate in a way a crew neck does not, and cotton knit at 10 gauge holds its shape without being warm enough to be uncomfortable indoors. It is the most requested piece in the register and the hardest to find sound, because cotton knits stretch at the neck and never come back.

The boots are sold with a matching belt because they came out of the same wardrobe and have aged to the same tone. Splitting them would have made two mediocre listings instead of one good one.

Boatneck record · Boot and belt record

What does this go with is a harder question than what does this look like, and it is the only one a single-run wardrobe can answer with a photograph.

Curatorial note

Read it as a dated record

This intake was plated across two afternoons in September. Sixteen of the pieces shown have one owner ahead of them and no second unit behind them, so the set will thin out over the next few weeks and we will not restock it. When a look goes, it stays gone, and the page keeps the plate as evidence that the combination existed.

If a piece here has sold and you want something close, write and say which look and which garment. We will tell you honestly whether anything in the register comes near it, and we will say so plainly when nothing does.
