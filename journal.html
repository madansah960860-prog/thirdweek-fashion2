The register, in longer form

Writing from the plate room

Three long pieces about photographing, mending and keeping old clothes, written by the people who do the work. They appear when there is something worth saying, not on a schedule.

Each one runs about ten minutes. Each one began as a question that arrived by email often enough to deserve a proper answer, so we wrote the answer once and now we point at it.

Why we keep writing it down

Learned by ruining things

Almost everything we know about buying, cleaning and keeping old clothes was paid for with a garment. A merino turtleneck that went through a machine on a wool cycle and came out a child's size. A waxed jacket sent to a dry cleaner, which stripped the finish for good. A leather bag conditioned with the wrong product and left blotchy for a year.

Those lessons cost money once. Writing them out means nobody has to buy them twice. If one article stops one person putting a Shetland through a warm wash, it has covered the afternoon it took to write.

Nothing here is sponsored, nothing carries an affiliate link, and no article is written to move a particular piece. Where a garment is named it is because that garment is the example, and the link goes to its record so the claim can be checked against the plate.

House line

One garment. One size. One owner next.

That sentence is the whole editorial policy. A single-run wardrobe cannot be described in adjectives, because there is no second unit to compare it against. It has to be described in measurements, in condition notes and in plates, and the articles above are an attempt to explain how each of those is made.
