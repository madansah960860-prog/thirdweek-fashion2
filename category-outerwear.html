Nine plates · Outerwear

The outer layer

Nine coats and jackets, each a single unit. Shell fabric, lining, closure and cloth weight sit on every record, because outerwear is bought on warmth before it is bought on shape.

Shell weight on every plate One garment, one owner next 30-day returns

Outerwear is the hardest category to buy secondhand and the easiest to get wrong. A coat photographs immaculately and still turns out to be a size you cannot raise your arms in, or a cloth too thin to be any use below forty degrees. Three numbers settle most of that, and we take all three ourselves: chest across the front laid flat, sleeve from the centre back of the collar to the cuff, total length from the base of the collar to the hem.

The cloth goes on the scale as well. A wool overcoat at 32 to 36 ounces per yard is a genuine winter garment. The same coat cut in 20 ounce cloth is a transitional jacket that will disappoint you in January. Both are worth owning. They are not the same purchase, and the record says which one you are reading.

Zips, snaps and buttons are worked twenty times before a piece is listed. Anything that sticks is serviced on the bench, or the fault is written into the description in plain language. Missing buttons are replaced with the closest match we can find and the swap is stated. A full button set is never replaced to make a garment look newer than it is.

Every coat here was dry cleaned or laundered before it went under the lights, and waxed cotton was rewaxed where the finish had gone patchy. The date of that work appears in the record. If you would rather have the original finish left alone, say so before you order and we will note it.

Method

How we grade a shell

Three things decide whether a coat earns its price: what the cloth weighs, whether the hardware still works, and what has been done to the finish. All three go into the record before the plate is published.

Weight, not look

The cloth goes on the scale and the approximate ounce weight is stated on every listing. Above 30 oz is a genuine winter coat. Between 16 and 24 oz is a three-season jacket. A 34 oz Melton and a 20 oz overcoat can look identical in a photograph and behave nothing alike in January.

Twenty turns of the hardware

Every zip, snap and button is worked twenty times before a piece is listed. Anything that sticks is serviced on the bench or the fault is written into the description in plain language. Missing buttons are replaced with the closest match we can find, and we say which one is new.

Finish, with a date

Each coat is dry cleaned or laundered before it goes under the lights, and waxed cotton is rewaxed where the finish had gone patchy. The date of that work sits in the record. If you would rather keep the original finish untouched, tell us before you order and we will note it.

Before you order

Four things to settle first

  • Measure a coat you already own

    Lay it flat, close it, and measure straight across the chest an inch below the armhole. Compare that number with ours, not your body measurement.

  • Decide on the layer underneath

    Two inches of extra chest is the difference between a coat over a shirt and a coat over a heavy sweater. Tell us which you plan to do and we will say whether it works.

  • Read the cloth weight

    Every listing states the approximate ounce weight. Above 30 oz is a genuine winter coat. Between 16 and 24 oz is a three-season jacket.

  • Know the return terms

    Thirty days from delivery. Exchanges ship both ways on us. Read the returns process before you buy, not after.
