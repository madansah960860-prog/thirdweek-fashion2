How a price is set

Four inputs, no calendar

A price comes from four things: what the piece cost us, what cleaning and repair cost, the hours spent measuring and plating it, and how rare that garment is in that size. Nothing else moves the number. There is no seasonal markdown calendar, because a coat that fits you in April still fits you in November.

If a piece sits unsold for ninety days we photograph it again and reconsider the price once. That revision is permanent. It is not a sale event with a clock attached.

How the register is built

What we cannot reorder

There is no second one

Stock here is not a quantity. It is a list of individual garments, each measured once and written into the record under its own plate number. Waitlists on a sold piece would be a courtesy with nothing behind it, so we do not keep them.

A want list is the honest version. Send us the category, the size range and the fabric you are after, and we check against it during intake. No charge, no obligation to buy, and no email unless something actually matches.

Add yourself to the want list

After a sale

The number retires with the garment

The moment a piece is paid for, its listing closes. The page does not go grey or say sold out, because there is nothing left to hold. It comes off the register and the plate number goes with it.

Plate numbers are never reissued. VS-0431 will belong to one chore jacket for as long as these records exist, and the next garment through the studio takes the next number in the run. That is what keeps the count on this page honest: twenty-seven listings means twenty-seven garments, each waiting for one person.
